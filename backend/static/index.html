    How it works

    1. We take a set of frames spread across the whole video.
    2. A face detector (MTCNN) finds each face and crops it out.
    3. A model (EfficientNet-B0) trained on real and fake clips from the FaceForensics++ dataset gives each face a fake probability.
    4. The frame scores become one verdict, and we point out the part of the video that looked the most suspicious.

    What the verdicts mean

    • Real: average fake probability under 40%.
    • Suspicious: between 40% and 70%. Worth a closer look.
    • High risk: over 70%.

    Good to know

    • This is a second opinion, not proof.
    • It only looks at faces. If no face is found, there is nothing to score.
    • The model learned from one family of face swap methods, so newer tools, heavy compression or bad lighting can fool it.
    • Your history is tied to your account. Other users cannot see it.
    • If we got it wrong, use the feedback buttons on a result.
